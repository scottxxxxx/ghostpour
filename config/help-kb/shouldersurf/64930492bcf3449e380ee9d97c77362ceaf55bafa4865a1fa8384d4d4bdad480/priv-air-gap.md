Shoulder Surf runs on your phone, beside the meeting, not inside it.

**What that means in practice.** Nothing is installed on your work laptop. The app doesn't join the call, doesn't appear in the participant list, and can't show up in a screen share, because it's not on the machine that's sharing. It works the same for a Zoom call, a Teams call, and six people in a room.

**The optional companions bend this a little, on purpose.** The Mac and Windows Companions are small apps you install on your own computer to feed call audio straight to the phone. They capture system audio and the mic locally and send it to your phone over your Wi-Fi; nothing goes to a meeting platform or to Shoulder Surf's servers. The companions' answer window is built to stay out of screen shares: Windows leaves it out of every capture, and on a Mac it's marked private, which kept it out of shares and screenshots in our tests. On a Mac the meeting app has the final say, so share a window rather than the whole screen if you want to be certain. The companions are optional; the phone works on its own.

**The part the air gap doesn't cover is consent.** Recording people is regulated differently in different places, and the app can't know where you are or who's in the room. When you first set up the app you agree that you're responsible for complying with recording consent laws where you are. The iPhone Mic card says it plainly: others are being recorded here, so tell them. The USB-C and companion modes record the far end of a call too; get their consent first. Read the screen mode exists for the case where you'd rather nothing be recorded: the camera reads the platform's live captions and no audio is stored.

The recording consent notice is in Settings > Privacy & About, next to the Terms of Service.
