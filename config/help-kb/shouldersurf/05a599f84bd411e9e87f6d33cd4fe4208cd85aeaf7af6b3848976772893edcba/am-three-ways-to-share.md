The ⋯ menu on a meeting offers two different things. "What's the Difference?" in the same menu says it in the app; here it is in full.

**Share Meeting (a link).** The whole meeting goes up to Shoulder Surf's servers and you get a link. Someone with the app taps it and the meeting opens right in their Review, where they can translate it. Someone without the app sees the report on a web page. You pick what travels: queries and chat, the report, AI-generated files, photos, audio. The transcript always travels. The link expires (you choose: 1, 7, 30, 90 or 365 days), and you can revoke it any time with Stop Sharing Link. Anyone with the link can open it, no sign-in. You need to be signed in to make one. See "Sharing a link."

**Export Artifacts (plain files).** Ordinary files: the transcript as .txt, subtitles as .srt, the audio as .m4a, the summary, the report as HTML, your queries and chat as HTML, the photos, any combination. They go through the iOS share sheet to Mail, Dropbox, WhatsApp, Files, wherever. Nothing is hosted, no app is needed to open them, and it's a copy: it never updates and can't be revoked. You can pick a language for the text files before sending. Above a few megabytes the app suggests AirDrop.

**A shared meeting includes the full transcript.** Anything you send is a copy on the recipient's side. Only share what you'd be comfortable with them keeping.

Send as Meeting File (a .shouldersurf file) was removed in November 2026: a file sent over Messages couldn't open the app, so Share Meeting does that job now. A .shouldersurf file you already have still opens in Shoulder Surf.

There's also an iMessage app: once a meeting has a share link, it shows up in the Shoulder Surf app drawer in Messages as a card you can drop into a chat. Links are made in the app, not in Messages.
