**What the app records with.** In the default iPhone Mic mode, it always uses the phone's built-in microphone, even when AirPods are connected. AirPods are for listening to answers, not for picking up the room. (Their mics are narrow-band and tuned for your voice, which is exactly what you don't want when you're trying to hear six people.) There's no setting to pick a different mic; the listening mode is the only control. See "How should I listen?"

**What you hear answers through.** Any Bluetooth headphones or headset. The app never grabs AirPods away from your Mac or iPad on its own; if they're paired elsewhere, use the route button beside Play or Control Center. Nothing is read aloud until you press Play. Details in "Hearing answers through AirPods."

**Phone speaker.** Off by default, because the mic would hear the answer and put it in your transcript. Turn it on in Settings > Audio > Output > Speaker Playback if you're alone and headphone-free. Wired headphones go through this same switch.

**Voice Isolation.** iOS has a Mic Mode (in Control Center) that filters out every voice but yours. Great for calls, terrible for recording a room. If it's on, the idle screen shows a yellow "Voice Isolation is on" note with a Change button that opens the system picker. Pick Standard or Wide Spectrum so everyone gets captured.

**Placement.** The phone hears what the mic hears. Put it on the table, screen up, within a few feet of the people or the laptop speakers. If you're on a video call, turn the laptop volume up. A "Move closer or turn up volume" pill shows at the start of a session if the signal is weak.

**Muting.** The mic circle at the top right mutes during a meeting. Muted audio is dropped from the recording and the transcript.
