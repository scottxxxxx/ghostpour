The small pill under the orb says how the app will hear the meeting. Tap it to switch; hold it for the full explainer ("How I listen"). You can also change it from the top of the recording screen mid-meeting.

**iPhone Mic** (iPad Mic on an iPad). No hardware. The built-in mic hears the room and your laptop speakers. Use it when people are with you, or the call is playing out loud. Everyone in earshot is being recorded, so tell them. Start here if you're not sure.

**Shoulder Surf Companion.** Best audio. A small free app on your Mac or Windows PC sends the call's own audio straight to the phone: both sides of the conversation, nothing through the air, no volume to keep up. If the computer isn't reachable when you start, or drops mid-meeting, the phone mic takes over on its own. See the Mac and Windows companion articles.

**USB-C.** For when you can't install anything on your computer and don't want the call playing out loud. It needs three pieces of hardware: a JK Audio CellTap 4C, any 3.5 mm headset, and a USB-C to 3.5 mm four-pin cable. Your headset and the caller both arrive through the cable, and the phone mic stays off. The caller is being recorded, so get consent. Niche, and the card in the app walks through it.

**Read the screen.** No audio at all. Point the back camera at the meeting's live captions on your laptop screen and the app reads them. Accuracy follows whatever the captions say. Use it when you'd rather nothing be recorded.

The app checks what each mode needs before it starts: mic permission, the cable being plugged in, the companion being connected. A mode that can't run right now says why ("Connect your CellTap 4C to use," "Not connected to your computer").

**Can't record this time?** The explainer ends with "Import a Recording": record on your Apple Watch, iPhone, or the meeting platform itself, then bring the file into Review.
