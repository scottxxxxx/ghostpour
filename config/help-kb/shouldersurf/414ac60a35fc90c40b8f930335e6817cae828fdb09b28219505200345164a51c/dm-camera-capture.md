The camera is on while you record, pointed at whatever's in front of the phone: a shared screen, a whiteboard, a slide. It's there for two reasons: so you can snap what you're seeing, and so the AI can look at it when you ask.

**Taking a photo.** Tap anywhere on the camera view. A flash and a thumbnail confirm it. Pinch to zoom first if you need to. The **Photos** button appears in the toolbar once you have at least one, with a count. Tap it to browse, swipe between photos, save, crop or delete.

**Using a photo in a question.** In the Query sheet, under Attach Photos, pick from this session's photos (or your Photo Library). The photo goes with that question only. Quick Prompts can also be set to take a photo automatically when you tap them (Settings > Quick Prompts > the prompt's capture switch).

**Turning the camera off.** Tap the green camera button at the top right. It goes red and stops for this meeting. Whether the next meeting starts with it on is up to Settings > Camera > "When a meeting starts" (On, the default, Off, or Same as last meeting). The first time, a card explains that photos give the AI something to look at. The phone also turns the camera off on its own if it gets hot (see "When the phone gets hot").

**With the Mac Companion.** The camera button asks "What am I looking at?" instead: the back camera, one of your Mac's screens ("Entire screen," "Call is here," or a cropped part of a screen you set once), or "Nothing visual" to save battery. Captures then come from the Mac screen rather than the room, and the HUD reads "Watching <screen>."

**Read the screen mode.** A different thing entirely: no audio is recorded, and the back camera reads the meeting's live captions off your laptop screen. Pick it from the listening mode pill. Accuracy follows whatever the captions say.

Photos are stored with the meeting on your phone (and in your private iCloud if sync is on). They're only sent to an AI when you attach them to a question, and only to the model you picked.
