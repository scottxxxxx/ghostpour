Answers never read themselves out loud. You press Play. That's deliberate: the app sits beside your meeting, it shouldn't start talking into it.

**Where the controls are.** Every answer has a transport bar: Restart, Play/Pause, a speed control (tortoise to hare: 1x, 1.2x, 1.5x, 2x), an output route picker, and the cards switch. While it reads, the sentence being spoken is highlighted and the text scrolls along.

**What it plays through.** By default, Play only works when a Bluetooth output is connected (AirPods, a headset, CarPlay). With nothing connected, Play is simply greyed out. If you'd rather hear answers from the phone itself, turn on Settings > Audio > Output > **Speaker Playback**. The footer warns you why it's off by default: with the mic unmuted, speaker audio can be picked up and land in your transcript. Wired headphones count as the speaker path, so they need Speaker Playback on too.

**AirPods on another device.** The app won't yank your AirPods away from your Mac or iPad. iOS treats a recording app like a phone call and doesn't let it steal audio from whatever else is playing. If Play is greyed out while your AirPods are connected to something else, use the route picker beside Play (or Control Center) to point them at the iPhone.

**USB-C cable mode.** Answers are never sent down the cable. They go to the speaker if Speaker Playback is on, otherwise they're skipped.

**Voice and language.** The voice is a system voice chosen in Settings > Audio > Text-to-Speech > Voice. Read-aloud voices are English only today, even for Spanish, French or Japanese meetings. Download more voices in iOS Settings > Accessibility.

**Speed.** Changing speed restarts the current sentence at the new rate. Until you touch it, 1x follows your iOS Spoken Content rate.

If VoiceOver is running, the app doesn't read answers aloud, so the two don't talk over each other. Closing the answer sheet stops playback.
